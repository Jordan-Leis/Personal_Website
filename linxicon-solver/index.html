How the solver works

Words have neighbors

Each word has a position in a 300-dimensional space learned from language and common knowledge. Similar directions usually mean related words. The map squashes that space into two dimensions: a useful sketch, not an exact measurement.

The search fans out

Breadth-first search looks one link away, then two, and so on, so the first chain it finds has the fewest added words. Among chains of the same length, the solver prefers the highest total link score.

The game has the final say

WordNet relationships can strengthen a link beyond its vector similarity, and the game's scores can still disagree with the local model. The final candidates are checked against the game's own scores and replayed under its top-five link rule before a chain is marked verified.

Method, limits and data credits

The replay plays back recorded search events at an adjustable pace. Discovery counts come from the full local graph; at most 500 words and a bounded set of links are drawn. Contours show the density of drawn points. Cosine scores are computed in the original vector space. Only the bundled daily neighborhood is navigable.

A verified chain means every word passed the game's dictionary check and a board replay using captured game scores connected the two starters. It does not mean a browser win screen was observed. "Shortest" is limited to the selected vocabulary and local model; the game's own behavior may admit another chain.
