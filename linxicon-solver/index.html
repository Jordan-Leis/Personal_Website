03 / READING THE ATLAS

Meaning has
more than one route.

I.

Words have neighbors.

Each word has a position in a 300-dimensional space, learned from language and common knowledge. Similar directions often mean related words. This map compresses that space into two dimensions: a useful sketch, not an exact measurement.

II.

The search fans out.

Breadth-first search looks one link away, then two, and so on. It finds routes with the fewest added words. Among routes of equal length, the solver prefers the highest total link score.

III.

The game has the final say.

WordNet relationships can strengthen a link beyond its vector similarity. The game's scores can still disagree with our model. We check the final candidates and replay its top-five link rule before marking a route verified.

Method, limitations & data credits

The animation replays recorded events at an adjustable pace. Discovery counts come from the full local graph; at most 500 words and a bounded edge set are drawn. Map contours show density of displayed points. Exact cosine scores are computed in the original vector space. Only the bundled daily neighborhood is navigable.

A server-verified route means its words passed validation and a board replay using captured server scores connected the starters. It does not mean a browser win screen was observed. “Optimal” is limited to the selected vocabulary and local model; server-only behavior may admit another route.
