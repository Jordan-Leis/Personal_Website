I like to build.

                        
I'm a 3B Electrical Engineering student at the University of Waterloo. I've built at every layer I've needed to: an RL controller and the paper behind it, RAG pipelines over 300-page regulatory documents, a Qt/C++ motion-control app, PCBs through EMI/EMC compliance, and now pipelined fixed-point accelerators in SystemVerilog. Not to collect skills. The problem kept moving.


                        
I got here from the other direction. I spent two years doing AI research at Wat.AI, leading an 11-person team building a reinforcement learning controller for off-grid microgrids in rural sub-Saharan Africa. It worked: 23% less diesel per year at equal reliability, and a first-authored paper accepted at CUCAI 2026.


                        
Then I looked at deploying it. The low-cost board we needed to run the controller had roughly doubled in price and was unavailable, squeezed by the AI compute shortage. The inference compute cost more than parts of the microgrid it was supposed to control. The algorithm was never the bottleneck. The hardware was. And when compute gets scarce, the people with the least get cut off first.


                        
I called it a pivot. It isn't. I started at the application, hit a wall made of hardware, and went one layer down to work on the wall.


                        
Right now the bottleneck is hardware, so that's where I am: working through the ECE 124 → ECE 222 → ECE 327 Digital Hardware Systems sequence, closing timing through architecture instead of tool flags. If the bottleneck moves again, I'll move with it. Before this: ML Engineer (Research) at Health Canada's PMRA; an experiment I designed at the National Research Council with roughly $100,000 of lab equipment specced for it; TAing ECE 150 for 400+ first-years.


                        
Cansbridge Fellow, 1 of 15 selected nationally — Asia internship Winter 2027. VP Services, Waterloo Engineering Society.
